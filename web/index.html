§01 · Problem Statement

Why Multi-Session Workplace AI Companions Drift, Forget, or Hallucinate Stale Habits

Enterprise employees collaborate with an AI companion across weeks of evolving meetings—projects hand off, SQL warehouses migrate, and executive review templates change mid-quarter.

3 Failure Modes Measured on Bedrock Nova
In Plain English The 20-Second Mental Model

Imagine hiring a sharp chief of staff. If they wake up with total amnesia every morning, you waste the first ten minutes re-explaining which team you lead, which SQL dialect your data lake uses, and how your VP likes status memos formatted. Conversely, if they carry around a raw binder of every transcript from six months ago without crossing out old assignments, they will write queries against a decommissioned Redshift cluster for a project you handed off last month. QuickMemory gives the AI a living Personal Knowledge Graph: when your role or preferences change, old links are explicitly marked SUPERSEDED rather than deleted or confused with today's reality—and if you ask it to forget something sensitive, it prunes that node and its edges immediately.

FAILURE 01 · COLD-START AMNESIA

Stateless Sessions Score 6.9% Recall

Without persistent memory, every fresh session forgets which project the user owns, their SQL dialect, and their WBR format—forcing repetitive re-prompting and scoring only 6.9% slot recall on live us.amazon.nova-lite-v1:0.

FAILURE 02 · TRANSCRIPT STUFFING

100.0% Stale Context Exposure & O(S) Cost

Concatenating raw multi-session logs scales linearly with session count (1,180 avg input tokens across 7 sessions) and exposes superseded Q3 facts (Payments-v1, Redshift SQL) on 100.0% of prompts, achieving 0.0% contradiction-clean accuracy.

FAILURE 03 · FLAT VECTOR CHUNKS

75.0% Stale Drift & Missing 2-Hop Context

Top-K cosine chunk retrieval co-retrieves outdated Session 01 chunks alongside current ones (75.0% stale contamination, 25.0% contradiction accuracy) and misses 2-hop KPIs/blockers not lexically named in the user's prompt (65.5% recall).

STEP 01 · EPISODIC CONSOLIDATION

Typed Entity & Relation Extraction

Parses work transcripts into Person, Project, Metric, System, Preference, and Commitment nodes connected via typed edges (OWNS, PREFERS_FORMAT, BLOCKED_BY, COLLABORATES_WITH).

STEP 02 · CONTRADICTION GATE

Deterministic SUPERSEDES Lineage

Detects slot conflicts (e.g., Payments-v1 → Checkout-Latency-Q4, Redshift → Athena Iceberg SQL), transitions prior edges to SUPERSEDED, and records explicit SUPERSEDES provenance.

STEP 03 · 2-HOP CONTEXT PACKER

Sub-Millisecond Hybrid Subgraph

Scores active nodes with BM25 + 3-gram cosine + Ebbinghaus retention exp(-λt / (1 + 0.48 ln(1+n))), expands 2-hop active edges in 0.624 ms p50 / 1.029 ms p99, and packs compact triples (-49.5% input tokens).

STEP 04 · BEDROCK & GOVERNANCE

Cited Companion + Right-to-Forget

Calls live us.amazon.nova-lite-v1:0 with inline [MEM-xx] & [EDGE-xx] citations while giving users instant Pin (locks retention at 100%) and Forget tombstoning (6/6 = 100.0% prune verified).

§03 · Implementation

Core Subsystems — Plain-English Intuition & Under-the-Hood Mechanics

Zero-dependency Python 3.12 Personal Knowledge Graph engine (quick_memory/graph_engine.py) and CloudFront /api/* serverless router (quick_memory/server.py), paired with human-readable intuition and exact implementation details.

10/10 Unit Tests Passing
SUBSYSTEM A · CONSOLIDATION graph_engine.py :: ingest_live_transcript()

1. Episodic-to-Semantic Entity & Edge Consolidation

Plain-English Intuition: Raw chat logs are noisy diaries; a Personal Knowledge Graph is a structured index card box. Instead of saving whole paragraphs verbatim, QuickMemory distills each meeting into clean cards—Projects you own, People you partner with, Metrics you chase, Systems blocking you, and Preferences you care about—connected by labeled arrows.
Under the Hood: Splits multi-turn transcripts into speaker-stripped clauses and runs ordered structural extractors producing MemoryNode dataclasses across 6 entity classes (Person, Project, Metric, System, Preference, Commitment) and 7 directed MemoryEdge types (OWNS, PREFERS_FORMAT, BLOCKED_BY, TRACKS_METRIC, COLLABORATES_WITH, COMMITTED_TO, SUPERSEDES). Re-mentions reinforce access_count += 2 and append session IDs to provenance_sessions.
SUBSYSTEM B · SUPERSESSION graph_engine.py :: upsert_entity_with_relation()

2. Deterministic Temporal Contradiction Gate

Plain-English Intuition: When you move to a new apartment, you forward your mail—you don't want packages shipped to both your old address and your new one. Whenever a user switches primary projects or replaces Redshift with Athena Iceberg SQL, QuickMemory retires the old link as SUPERSEDED while keeping an audit trail of when and why it changed.
Under the Hood: Functional preference and ownership edges carry a canonical slot_key (primary_project, sql_dialect, code_stack, doc_format, active_blocker). On upsert, any active edge matching (source_id, relation, slot_key) or an explicit contradicts_label transitions atomically to status="SUPERSEDED", records superseded_in_session, and emits an explicit SUPERSEDES directed lineage edge—driving stale prompt contamination to 0.0%.
SUBSYSTEM C · RETENTION graph_engine.py :: compute_ebbinghaus_retention()

3. Frequency-Reinforced Ebbinghaus Memory Decay

Plain-English Intuition: A one-off side comment from five weeks ago shouldn't carry the same weight as a formatting rule you rely on every Tuesday. Memories naturally fade over time unless repeated use reinforces them—or unless you explicitly click Pin to lock a non-negotiable habit at 100% forever.
Under the Hood: Evaluates continuous retention via R(t, n) = exp(-λ · age_days / (1 + 0.48 · ln(1 + access_count))) with default λ = 0.045. Every live 2-hop retrieval increments access_count on matched active nodes, flattening their decay slope over a 60-day horizon; pinned=True clamps R = 1.0 and forgotten=True clamps R = 0.0.
SUBSYSTEM D · RETRIEVAL & PRIVACY graph_engine.py + server.py

4. 2-Hop Hybrid Subgraph Packer & O(deg(v)) Tombstoning

Plain-English Intuition: If you ask "Draft my WBR update," a keyword search only looks for the word "WBR." A graph walks two steps outward from you: Step 1 finds your active project (Checkout-Latency-Q4), and Step 2 automatically pulls in that project's SLA target (180ms) and blocker (LakeFormation)—even though you never typed either word!
Under the Hood: Combines BM25 IDF (`k1=1.2, b=0.75`) + character 3-gram cosine similarity weighted by 0.55 + 0.45·R(t,n) to pick Hop-1 seeds, attaches active Preference nodes, expands Hop-2 active edges in 0.624 ms p50 / 1.029 ms p99, and serializes compact triples (`596` avg Bedrock inputTokens). Calling DELETE /api/memory/forget sets TOMBSTONED and marks incident active edges PRUNED in O(deg(v)) time (6/6 = 100.0% verified).
§04 · Design Tradeoffs

Architectural Tradeoffs & Production Engineering Decisions

Every memory architecture trades off token footprint, multi-hop recall, write-path latency, and deletion semantics. Here is how QuickMemory balances each axis based on live Bedrock Nova measurements.

Measured Across 4 Baselines

1. 2-Hop PKG Triples vs. Flat Vector Chunks & Full Transcripts

  • Token Budget vs. Multi-Hop Completeness: Flat top-3 cosine chunks use fewer input tokens (329 vs. 596 for QuickMemory), because they only send 3 short snippets. However, flat chunks miss 2-hop blockers/KPIs and co-retrieve superseded Q3 turns—dropping recall to 65.5% and exposing stale context on 75.0% of queries. QuickMemory invests ~267 extra tokens for structured 2-hop triples to achieve 89.7% recall (+24.2 pts) and 0.0% stale contamination, while still saving 49.5% vs. full transcript stuffing (1,180 tokens).
  • Asymptotic Scaling Across Months of Work: Full-history stuffing grows as O(S) with total sessions S; QuickMemory's prompt envelope scales as O(|V_active_2hop|)—bounded by the user's active projects and preferences regardless of whether S = 10 or S = 500.

2. Synchronous Clause Extraction vs. Async LLM Consolidation & Tombstones

  • Write-Path Latency vs. Open-Vocabulary Nuance: Running an LLM call on every session write adds ~1.5–3.0s latency and extra token spend. QuickMemory uses a fast deterministic clause/slot extractor on the synchronous write path (`< 1 ms` consolidation) for zero-wait interactive session ingestion, while pairing easily with asynchronous background LLM entity Linking for ambiguous prose at scale.
  • In-Place O(deg(v)) Tombstoning vs. Vector Re-Indexing: Deleting a single person or project from raw chunk embeddings leaves partial co-references scattered across overlapping sliding-window chunks. Graph node tombstoning (`TOMBSTONED` + `PRUNED` incident edges) severs retrieval paths atomically in `O(deg(v))` without re-embedding transcripts.
